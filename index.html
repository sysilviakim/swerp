Who we are

Based in Seoul, SWERP offers an online forum for research projects in political science. Our aim is to provide researchers an opportunity to present in-progress research, receive feedback, and improve their projects. Professors, researchers, and advanced graduate students are encouraged to attend and participate. We welcome all research topics related to political science, including but not limited to international relations, comparative politics, Korean politics, and American politics.

If you are interested in presenting or participating in SWERP, please email Brandon Ives at bives@snu.ac.kr.

Linguistic Diversity and Violence: Evidence from China's Cultural Revolution

Presenter: Jieun Kim, Assistant Professor, Seoul National University

[Abstract]

Can preexisting social cleavages intensify conflict even when they do not define its principal divide? I examine this question through linguistic cleavages within the Han majority during China's Cultural Revolution, a political upheaval not organized along linguistic lines. Combining an original county-level language dataset with Andrew Walder's China Political Events Dataset (1966–1971), I show that multiple-dialect counties experienced substantially higher death tolls than single-dialect counties. Event-level analysis further reveals that linguistic diversity was not associated with conflict onset but with its escalation. The death-toll gap emerged in late 1967 amid the collapse of political authority, before the wave of large-scale deaths associated with state repression. These findings underscore that politically non-salient but socially meaningful cleavages can intensify conflict without necessarily triggering it, when local social structures have room to matter.
